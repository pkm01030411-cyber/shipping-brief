Pressure builds on well-connected Russian businessman who wants to buy Norwegian Arctic copper mine

Artem Volynets maintains that he "has no connections with anyone in Russia" and that he has not been in the country "for a very, very long time." But data obtained by the Barents Observer show that he visited Russia at least eight times in 2021.

Atle Staalesen Atle Staalesen
17 November 2026 - 15:05

Volynets, who served as the right hand of Russian oligarch Oleg Deripaska for at least six years, is trying to buy the Nussir copper mine in Repparfjord, as well as a copper and zinc mine in Sulitjelma. The acquisition of the two northern Norwegian mines has created an outcry in the Nordic country, where several members of parliament are now calling for a cancellation of the deal. They argue that Volynets and his company could pose a risk to national security.

The Nussir mine has the biggest known copper reserves in Norway and is regarded as a strategic resource by the EU.

According to Volynets, he no longer has any business connections in Russia.

"I withdrew from the business world in Russia 13 years ago. I have no connections with anyone in Russia, and I haven’t been there for a very, very long time," he told Norwegian newspaper Finansavisen on 3 November 2026.

The Barents Observer can now reveal that Artem Volynets visited Russia at least eight times in 2021.

Information from a leaked database of travel data shows that the businessman travelled repeatedly between London and Moscow and St Petersburg. He also entered the country from Paris, Vienna, Warsaw, and Istanbul. The Barents Observer has not found information about visits to the country after 24 February 2022, when Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

Data also show that the businessman maintains an active account on Gosuslugi, the official Russian state services portal. Apparently, he is also a user of CryptoPro, a key Russian cryptographic service provider.

The account on Gosuslugi indicates that Volynets remains a Russian citizen with activities in Russia.

The Barents Observer has sent a request for comment to Artem Volynets via his press service. We asked about his travel to Russia, as well as about his registration on Gosuslugi and his relationship with Oleg Deripaska. At the time of this article's publication, he had not responded.

Artem Volynets has played a key role in several of Russia’s most powerful companies for more than a decade. He was especially close to Oleg Deripaska, the oligarch who owns Rusal, the world’s second-largest aluminium company, as well as key stakes in several holding companies, among them EN+ Group and Basic Element.

In 2007, Volynets, then first vice-president of SUAL Holding, was instrumental in the merger between SUAL, Rusal, and parts of Glencore. One year later, in 2008, when he was a board member and Director of Strategy and Corporate Development at Rusal, he oversaw the company’s acquisition of a 25% stake in Norilsk Nickel. In 2010, he led the preparations for Rusal’s IPO on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, making Rusal the first Russian company to be listed there.

Artem Volynets served as general director of the EN+ Group for three years. He was a board member of Norilsk Nickel until 2019.

He is a man known for his appetite for acquisitions and company expansion.

In an interview with the Financial Times in early 2026, he revealed that his company is working on as many as 10 potential deals to buy copper mines around the world. ACG seeks to become a "western supplier" of the in-demand red metal, he told the newspaper.

His company, ACG Metals, was previously named ACG Acquisition Co and was classified as a so-called Special Purpose Acquisition Company (SPAC).

Volynets is based in London and is not listed on any international sanctions list. He  emphasises to Norwegian media that he has cut all ties with the heavily sanctioned Oleg Deripaska and his companies.

"I have no links to business leaders in Russia or their companies at present, nor to any of those I have worked with in the past," he told VG.

ACG Metals is registered in the British Virgin Islands. Its two biggest owners are a company registered in the Cayman Islands and a Turkish conglomerate, according to VG.

Deripaska is on the sanctions lists of the EU, the USA, the UK, Norway, and several other countries. According to the EU Council, Deripaska is supporting, materially or financially, and benefitting from Russia’s military and industrial complex.

On 16 November 2026, Volynets paid a visit to the mine in Repparfjord. Journalists from VG were on site to cover the visit. The newspaper has repeatedly asked Volynets what he thinks about Russia’s war of aggression against Ukraine and whether Russia should pull out of the neighbouring country. According to the newspaper, Volynets has not responded to the questions.

The businessman is holding meetings also with local politicians in the municipality of Hammerfest, as well as in Oslo, this week.

Several Norwegian politicians are calling on the Norwegian government to put a stop to Volynets’ and ACG’s acquisition of the mines.

The Green Party argues that national security aspects must be taken into considerable account before ACG’s acquisition is approved. In a proposition to the Storting, the Norwegian parliament, the party calls for "a temporary suspension of ACG Metals’ acquisition of the Nussir mine until the Norwegian security authorities can confirm that the acquisition does not pose a security threat."

The proposition did not win sufficient support from the other parties.

In a comment to the Barents Observer, Siren Jensen, an MP from the Greens, emphasises that an acquisition of the Nussir mine by ACG is bound up with national security risks.

"The company leader’s Russian background and his repeated lies to Norwegian media have not assured us," Jensen says.

She is critical of the Norwegian government’s handling of the case.

"Who has conducted the security assessment of the company?" she asks. "Is it the government itself?" she adds.

Jensen, herself from Hammerfest, is also critical of local mayor Terje Rogde, who this week welcomed Artem Volynets at the Hammerfest town hall.

"It is absurd," she says about the meeting.

According to the MP, locals in Hammerfest are increasingly sceptical about the Nussir project.

"Local faith in the project has declined. People are less positive; they understand that the ripple effects are limited."
