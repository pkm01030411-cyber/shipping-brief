China secures another major energy contract in Central Asia

China-Central Asia Monitor

Katherine Hawley, Elisabeth Kokorin, Konrad Sartorius Nov 17, 2026
Kazakhstan’s giant Kashagan hydrocarbon field. QazaqGaz just signed a contract with a Chinese-led consortium to build a natural gas processing plant that will be fed by the field. (Photo: gov.kz)

QazaqGaz, Kazakhstan’s vertically integrated national gas company, has signed a contract with a Chinese consortium to build a gas processing plant with an annual capacity of 2.5 billion cubic meters (bcm) pumped from the Kashagan field. Under the turnkey arrangement, the consortium will manage every phase of the project from design to final operational testing. The exact value of the contract has not been disclosed, but in 2024, the project was estimated to cost upwards of $2.5 billion. The processing plant deal is the latest in a series of major wins for Chinese companies in the energy sector this year, including a $6.1 billion biorefinery project in Uzbekistan and several energy contracts in Kazakhstan. The Kashagan construction consortium comprises China National Chemical Engineering Sixth Construction Company, China Wuhuan Engineering Corporation, AVC Production, and C-NRG.

Central Asia

Construction ministers from China and all five Central Asian countries adopted two cooperation documents during the second China-Central Asia ministerial meeting in Bishkek. The documents include a joint statement and a 2027-28 action plan to expand cooperation in the construction and infrastructure development sectors. Discussions among the six countries included earthquake-resistant construction, affordable housing, digital building technologies, construction-materials production, and specialist training.  No specific new project financing or construction contracts were announced.

Astana hosted a roundtable titled The Role of Mediation in Strengthening International Security: The Path to Nuclear Disarmament, drawing representatives from China, India, and Kyrgyzstan. Kazakhstan joined the Convention on the Establishment of an International Mediation Organization in July 2026, and efforts are currently underway to ratify the associated documents. The roundtable produced recommendations formulated to advance international mediation and strengthen cooperation among experts.

Kazakhstan

Kazakhstan and China are expanding digital cooperation, China Daily reports. The Astana Data Exchange (ADX) and Shanghai Data Exchange (SDE) have commenced joint listings of data products. State-owned enterprises and private firms involved in finance and IT are likewise joining forces to collaborate on data markets, digital economy research, and smart city projects.

KazAvtoZhol Board Chairman Darkhan Imanashev announced the 1,100-kilometer Aktobe-Kyzylorda highway is being upgraded from two lanes to four.

Kazakhstan’s Chief Transport Prosecutor’s Office discovered that 92,000 tons of oil transported through the Kenkiyak-Kumkol and Atasu-Alashankou pipelines, key sections of the strategic Kazakhstan-China oil pipeline network, were unaccounted for in official records. Consequently, regulatory adjustments yielded an additional 2.9 billion tenge in corporate income tax and 300 million tenge in property tax, inclusive of penalties.

A cohort of 14 Kazakh specialists has begun training in China under the newly established Smart Water Management Program, a joint initiative between the Kazakh Ministry of Water and Irrigation and China Energy International Group. The curriculum focuses on advanced water purification technologies, digital management solutions, and hydraulic system operations.

Astana hosted a gala marking the end of the Kazakhstan-China Year of Culture, featuring performances by Chinese and Kazakh artists. High-ranking Kazakh and Chinese officials in attendance touted the depth of bilateral relations, adding that approximately 40 cross-cultural events took place across both nations this year. Beijing plans to open a China Cultural Center in Astana by 2027.

Uzbekistan

China has conducted a public relations campaign aimed at Uzbeks that touts Beijing as a beneficial partner. As reported by Eurasianet, CGTN, a broadcaster controlled by the Chinese Communist Party’s Central Propaganda Department, produced over 70 articles and videos in early November in both Uzbek and Russian. The items covered a wide range of political, economic, and socio-cultural topics.

Uzbek government agencies are sending representatives on a two-week training program in Beijing aimed at sharing Chinese public administration practices, including logistics and infrastructure investment, export and import operations, and education. In addition to Uzbek participants, officials from Azerbaijan, Belarus, Moldova, Tajikistan, and Turkmenistan are participating in the program. Furthermore, Uzbekistan’s Ministry of Agriculture held a briefing for employees on modernizing the sector using Chinese methods and technologies. The briefing was led by ministry officials who had just returned from a visit to the PRC. Such events signal growing Uzbek (and more broadly Eurasian) interest in emulating the Chinese model of economics and governance.

Representatives of Uzbekistan’s and China’s associations of customs brokers signed an MoU to “cooperate in the field of customs and brokerage activities, exchange expertise, and keep each other informed about changes in customs legislation, modern customs clearance methods, digitalization, and the simplification of foreign trade procedures.”

Uzbekistan’s Ferghana region opened its trade and investment center in the Chinese city of Qingdao. Regional officials also signed MoUs with the Jinan Committee for the Promotion of International Trade, as well as with several private companies.

Representatives of Uzbekistan’s state-owned water utility, Uzsuvtaminot, met with Chinese investors to discuss the construction of a drinking water treatment plant in Navoi. The proposed plant would be developed by China Energy Overseas, a privately owned firm.

Turkmenistan

Turkmen and Chinese companies signed 12 memoranda of understanding during a bilateral business forum held November 6-7 in Shanghai alongside the China International Import Expo. A Turkmen company, Ojar Aziya, concluded four memos, including one with Super Smart Home on smart-home and artificial intelligence technologies, and another with Link Global International Logistics covering international freight transportation. The forum attracted more than 500 government and business representatives. The aggregate potential value, target implementation timetables, and other key details were not immediately disclosed in the MoUs.

The Seyitnazar Seydi Turkmen State Pedagogical Institute conducted a nationwide Chinese language proficiency contest for students from 11 Turkmen higher education institutions. The institute has an agreement with the PRC’s Ministry of Education to promote Chinese language learning. Additionally, the Luban Workshop in Ashgabat hosted its first computer science contest for students from Turkmen and Chinese universities. Luban workshops attached to higher education institutions across Central Asia are a common tool used by China to project its influence.

Kyrgyzstan

Kyrgyzstan is considering building an elevated electric rail transit system in Bishkek to alleviate the city’s notorious traffic congestion. The mayor’s office reached an agreement with China’s BYD to form a working group to undertake a feasibility study.

Tajikistan

Bilateral trade turnover between Tajikistan and China reached just over $3 billion during the first nine months of 2026, an increase of over 53 percent compared to the same period the previous year, according to figures disclosed by Chinese chargé d’affaires Wan Guochun.
